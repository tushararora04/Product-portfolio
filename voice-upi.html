A voice- and regional-language payment experience that closes the gap between UPI’s promise and the people English-first, tap-heavy interfaces overlook.

Payments should not require a translation.

The opportunity was not to make UPI technically cleverer. It was to let people who are more comfortable speaking than reading complete a familiar payment with confidence, without weakening the security model that already works.

What I prioritised

Trust before novelty.

Research pointed to recognition accuracy and understandable security prompts as the two conditions for repeat use. Rather than introduce voice biometrics, I retained the UPI PIN as the authentication step in V1.

PRODUCT DECISION

Voice starts the transaction. PIN still approves it.

A deliberately constrained first release that makes the experience easier without asking users to trust a new security model.

How success is measured

A conversation that completes.

The main signals: voice transactions completed without manual fallback, repeat usage rate, and fraud parity with conventional PIN-based UPI.
